# VS Store Sales-First Product and Catalog Execution Plan

## Decision and objective

The commercial objective is to win more completed US orders while retaining a verified positive contribution on every changed product. “Aggressive selling” means a clear, convincing product page and a competitive, supportable delivered price—not a generic SEO rewrite, the highest possible markup, artificial discounts, or filling the catalog to a number. The execution order is: remove purchase-path friction, build product/customer trust, merchandise only the right items, then scale traffic only where the order economics work. Orders and contribution are the outcomes; page polish alone is not success. Shopify is the source of truth for catalog changes. Google Merchant Center, Google product feeds and Google-specific field changes are out of scope. The SALT repository's credentials, hostnames, tokens, theme settings and running processes are out of scope and must remain untouched. The requested web-repository deployment and separate Shopify-theme delivery are in scope only as final, independently verified delivery gates after the catalog release succeeds.

The work covers only products already in the Shopify catalog. Accurate existing titles/descriptions and good product-faithful images stay as they are. No new products will be sourced, added to DSers Import List, pushed, or created as part of this work. The DOCX named below has one role in this task: classify existing products into separate, evidence-matched family collections. It is not a sourcing, SEO-copy, stock-filter, or deletion instruction.

1. Freeze a complete, fresh Shopify read of existing product IDs/statuses, exact titles/types/categories/options, all product and variant images, and current collection membership. Never classify from the term list alone.
2. Treat the 10 DOCX lanes as umbrella classification labels, not as Shopify collection titles. Resolve each lane to exact existing Shopify collection ID(s), handle(s), and rule-driving values from a complete live collections read. The store has more-granular collections, so map by exact product function only; do not guess an alias, create/rename a collection, or use a fallback. If no defensible existing target exists, record that lane as unmapped and hold those products.
3. For each existing product, determine the canonical item and primary purpose from its exact image set, title, category, options/variant matrix, and verified attributes. Assign one primary DOCX family lane; then choose the exact existing shopper-facing collection(s) whose rules fit that item. Gendered lanes require evidence that the item itself is genuinely marketed/designated for that gender; supplier tags, generic audience labels, and phrases are not enough. A genuinely unisex item must not be forced into Women or Men.
4. Products that do not fit a lane, fit multiple lanes without a clear primary use, or have conflicting/missing identity evidence receive `hold` with the reason recorded. Preserve accurate existing collection memberships unless a verified mismatch and a field-bounded, exact-ID change plan support correction; never remove items from unrelated editorial/occasion collections by keyword sweep.
5. Run a 10-product pilot spanning distinct families and ambiguous edge cases as a proposal first. Independently inspect all evidence and collection IDs, then expand in deterministic 200-product proposal batches over the full existing catalog. Each record carries Product GID, status, source snapshot hash, current memberships, proposed family/collection GID, product/image/option evidence, confidence, rationale, and hold reason.
